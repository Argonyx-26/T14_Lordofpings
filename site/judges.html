Argus turns the cameras and sensors a site already owns into a handful of ranked, explained incidents, projects where each one is heading, and leaves every decision with a person. Everything below runs on real footage, and every number has its sample size and method next to it.

The tour

What to click

The live demo is a snapshot of the replay at 15:20, bundled with the page (any stand-in event is labelled in the console). The stage demo adds live footage, replay and uploads.

  1. Read the top band.

    What needs a decision now, the funnel from every signal to a human decision (325 → 21 → 3), and the score against ground truth.

  2. Open the top incident.

    The main camera follows it. The panel shows the brief, the evidence (click a row to replay the moment), and the response progress: signals, opened by Argus, your decision.

  3. Look at "Where this is heading".

    The crime-script stage it has reached and what the next stage would do to its risk, re-scored by the real scorer.

  4. Press "Plan the response".

    How incidents like this unfold, what would change the score (another sensor, night time, a stricter or looser site profile, a dismissal), and every response compared: time to effect, the stage it stops, people affected, disruption. Simulate one, then apply it.

  5. Read "Part of a pattern".

    Argus links incidents above the incident list: the same crime-script act, inside 30 minutes, with a walking-time check on the site map (walkable, same place, or too soon to walk: at least two people). The map draws the links, outlines where to watch next after a theft, and hatches the places no camera can see. It links behaviour, place and time, never faces or phones, and never changes a score.

  6. Respond.

    Acknowledge, escalate or dismiss. The decision goes into a hash-chained audit log, and a dismissal teaches Argus to score similar alerts lower here. Then Case report: the whole case, printable.

  7. On stage: analyse a video.

    Hand us any clip. Argus tracks it, flags threats, and returns a threat assessment under the profile you pick, with a forecast for each incident.

When the AI is wrong

A person decides. Always.

Incidents come from measured rules and a transparent score (severity × confidence × area criticality × corroboration × time of day × feedback), never from a language model. The model only writes the brief and answers questions; the evidence it cites, the action it recommends and the places it names are checked against the log, or the template brief is used. We tried an AI second opinion on camera alerts: it threw away four of six real thefts, so it stays out. Forecasts are the same scorer run on real evidence plus one hypothetical signal, and every assumption (guard posts, walking speed, police time) is labelled as one.

Privacy

Built to count, not to identify

DataWhat Argus does with it
FacesNo face recognition, no biometrics. People are tracked as anonymous boxes within one camera.
Phone locationsUsed only as counts per area (crowding, people leaving). Argus never follows a phone: positions become per-area counts in memory, no event carries a device id, and nothing about one phone is stored, shown or sent to a model. Never linked to the people on camera. The demo GPS is MEVA's consented actor data.
DecisionsEvery action is logged with role and time in an append-only, hash-chained log, so it can't be edited quietly.
FootageStays on the site's machine. The demo runs offline on one laptop.
